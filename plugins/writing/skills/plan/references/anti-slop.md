# Anti-Slop Writing Rules

Universal craft rules. These override anything else when in conflict.

## User-specific bans (from CLAUDE.md, non-negotiable)

1. No em-dashes (—), en-dashes (–), or hyphens as clause connectors. Hyphens in compound words are fine.
2. No slop words: delve, landscape, navigate, foster, tapestry, pivotal, crucial, robust, seamless, nuanced, multifaceted, underscore, testament, realm, elevate, streamline.
3. No lists formatted as `**Bold Label:** description`. Use plain lists or prose.
4. No "It's not just X, it's Y" or "less about X and more about Y". State the point directly.

## Orwell's six rules (Politics and the English Language, 1946)

1. Never use a metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, scientific word, or jargon if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

## Strunk and White, composition core

1. Choose a suitable design and hold to it.
2. Make the paragraph the unit of composition. One paragraph, one message.
3. Use the active voice.
4. Put statements in positive form.
5. Use definite, specific, concrete language.
6. Omit needless words.
7. Avoid a succession of loose sentences.
8. Express coordinate ideas in similar form.
9. Keep related words together.
10. Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end.

## Paul Graham, condensed (Writing, Briefly)

1. Write a bad version 1 fast. Rewrite it many times.
2. Cut hard. Most of the work is removal.
3. Write the way you talk to a friend.
4. Read aloud to find the sentences you dread reading.
5. Prefer short Germanic words over long Latinate ones.
6. Do not try to sound impressive. Write about what you actually care about.
7. The goal of an essay is to surprise. If you are not surprised while writing, neither is the reader.
8. Get feedback. Readers identify confusing or dragging sections better than you do.

## Plain-language defaults

1. Lead with the point. Topic sentence first, support after.
2. Concrete beats abstract. Numbers, names, specific verbs.
3. Sentences average under 20 words. Vary the rhythm.
4. Active voice unless the actor is unknown or genuinely irrelevant.
5. One idea per sentence. One message per paragraph.

## Self-test before sending

1. Does the first sentence say something?
2. Can a smart reader skim and still get the point?
3. Is there a sentence I would cut if I were ruthless? Cut it.
4. Did I use any banned slop word? Replace.
5. Did I use an em-dash or en-dash? Replace with a comma, period, or colon.
6. Is every claim either supported by evidence or framed honestly (`I think`, `probably`, `in my setup`)?
7. Did I auto-format the body as `**Bold:** description` bullets? Rewrite as prose or plain bullets.
8. Did I write "It's not just X, it's Y"? Rewrite.
